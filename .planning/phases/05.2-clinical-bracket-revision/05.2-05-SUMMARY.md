## Task 3 — Human browser pass — **COMPLETE, and it found a defect**

Andrew ran the pass on the deployed page and **rejected the first deploy**. The finding was not on
the checklist, because nobody had thought to put it there:

> With the `/60` denominator removed, the bare numeric score became *less* interpretable, not more.
> A `30` sat above a scale whose top band means `9+` — three times the threshold — with nothing left
> to explain its magnitude.

**This is the seventh defect on this project found by a human clicking and missed by a fully green
suite.** The phase had 759 passing tests, a clean typecheck, and six independently verified
served-bytes markers at the moment the defect was live. Keep the human browser pass.

### The fix — `ad9a550`

Removed from the patient-facing results entirely:
- the score circle (`quizResults__scoreCircle` / `__scoreNumber`)
- the `Score: {score}` readout (`scaleBar__value`)
- the score from the track's `aria-label` — a screen-reader user must not be told a number sighted
  users cannot see

What carries the result now: the `{zone} symptom burden` caption, the colour band, the bolded legend
word, and the recommendation copy.

Redeployed and re-verified on served bytes: 204,067 -> **203,686 B**, SHA-256 identical to the
committed artifact, `Score: ` and `, score ` both **0** (each measured 1-before), `/health` 200.
Confirmed visually on the deployed page. Andrew: **approved**.

### Two consequences recorded in code

1. **Interpolation became load-bearing.** The prior reasoning tolerated non-linear marker position
   *because the number carried the linear reading*. Both the number and the denominator are now
   gone, so marker position is the only thing distinguishing a patient at 9 from one at 60.
   Replacing it with a fixed per-zone position would now erase within-bracket ordering completely.
2. **Seam figures recomputed** for `0-2 / 3-8 / 9+`: score 8 at 66.67%, 9 at 67.31%, 10 at 67.95%.
   The boundary move relocated the seam from 6->7 to 8->9; it did not remove it.
